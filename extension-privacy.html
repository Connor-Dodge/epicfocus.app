Extension Privacy Policy

Epic Focus Blocker browser extension · Last updated: August 22, 2026

Epic Focus Blocker does not collect anything about you. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server of its own. It receives a list of sites to block from the Epic Focus desktop app running on your own computer, blocks them, and forgets them when the app disconnects.

This policy covers the browser extension only. The Epic Focus desktop and mobile apps have their own policy at epicfocus.app/privacy.html, which covers accounts, Apple Health, and calendar data. Nothing here applies to those; nothing there applies to the extension.

1. Who We Are

Epic Focus Blocker is published by Greatness Games LLC. Questions about this policy or about your data: connor@greatness.games.

2. What the Extension Collects

Nothing. Specifically, it does not collect, store, or transmit:

  • your browsing history or the pages you visit
  • the contents of any page
  • personally identifiable information of any kind
  • credentials, form data, or financial information
  • location, health, or activity data
  • any identifier that could be used to track you across sites or sessions

3. What It Does Hold, and Where

Your block list, in memory, while a session is running. The desktop app sends the domains and path patterns you chose to block. They live in the extension's memory as blocking rules, and they are deleted when the desktop app disconnects or the session ends. They are never written to disk by the extension and never leave your computer.

One timestamp, on your device. Local extension storage holds a single value — ef_lastSelfReloadAt — recording the last time the extension reloaded itself after noticing a version mismatch with the desktop app. It exists to stop a reload loop. It contains no browsing data and never leaves your device.

4. What It Talks To

The Epic Focus desktop app, on your own computer. The only connection the extension opens is a native-messaging connection to the Epic Focus native host (com.epicfocus.host) running locally. It is a connection between two programs on the same machine — nothing is sent over the internet. Over that connection the extension receives your block rules and reports back three things about itself so the app can tell a healthy setup from a broken one: its extension id, its version, and whether you have allowed it to run in private/incognito windows. It does not report what you browse.

The block page, when a block actually happens. A blocked navigation is redirected to epicfocus.app/blocked, a plain page hosted by us. That is an ordinary web request, so our web host records what any web host records — IP address, timestamp, and user-agent — and it tells us that some block page was loaded. It does not carry which site was blocked: the address the extension navigates to has no query string, no referrer data, and no identifier of any kind attached. We do not use these logs to build any profile, and we run no analytics or advertising scripts on that page.

5. Permissions, and Why Each Exists

  • declarativeNetRequest — to redirect blocked sites to the block page. It cannot read the contents of your requests.
  • nativeMessaging — to receive your block list from the Epic Focus desktop app on your own machine. This is the extension's only data source.
  • storage — for the single self-reload timestamp described above.
  • <all_urls> (host permission) — because you can choose to block any site on the web, the extension cannot know in advance which hosts it needs to act on. It is also what lets a small content script notice single-page-app navigations (for example youtube.com/shorts), which change the URL without loading a page. That script reads only the current URL and compares it against your own block patterns. It reads no page content and transmits nothing.

6. Data Sharing and Sale

We do not sell your data. We do not share it with third parties. We do not transfer it for advertising, credit assessment, lending, or any purpose unrelated to the extension's single purpose. There is no data to sell, share, or transfer.

7. Your Control

  • Blocking only happens while the Epic Focus desktop app is running and a Defenses profile is active. Ending a session or quitting the app removes every rule.
  • What is blocked is entirely your own list, edited in the desktop app.
  • Removing the extension removes everything it holds, including the one stored timestamp.

8. Children

The extension is not directed at children under 13 and collects no data from anyone, children included.
